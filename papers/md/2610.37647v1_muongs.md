# MuonGS: Muon with a Gram-Schmidt Tangent Projection

- Reference code: none linked from the paper source.
- Local implementation: [`muonc.py`](../../muonc.py) (`gs_projection=True`).

## Core Idea

The paper measures norm control by the *accumulated effective learning rate*

```text
C_T = sum_{t<T} eta_t / ||W_t||_F
```

For bounded update directions (`||O_t||_F <= M`), relative displacement over
any window is bounded by it: `||W_T - W_s|| / max(r_s, r_T) <= M (C_T - C_s)`,
and for a scale-invariant loss so is the attainable loss change. Sustained
exploration therefore needs `C_T` to keep growing late in training.

At constant nominal LR (`p = min(m, n)`, `r_t = ||W_t||_F`):

| Control | Norm bound | `C_T` | `C_2T - C_T` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vanilla Muon | `O(t)` | `Omega(log T)` (tight) | `Omega(1)` |
| MuonW (`1 - eta*lambda`) | `max(r_0, sqrt(p)/lambda)` | `Omega(T)` | `Omega(T)` |
| Fixed norm / MuonH | `R` | `eta T / R` | `eta T / R` |
| MuonGS | `O(sqrt t)` | `Omega(sqrt T)` | `Omega(sqrt T)` |

Vanilla Muon's norm can grow linearly because the orthogonalized direction has
a first-order radial component; raising the LR does not help because the norm
grows in proportion (their Prop. on learning-rate compensation). Bounded norms
are sufficient but not necessary: any growth `r_t <= r_0 + c t^a`, `a < 1`,
still gives polynomial accumulation.

## Step Math

```text
U_{t+1} = beta U_t + grad f(W_t)
O_t     = Orthogonalize(U_{t+1})                    # Newton-Schulz, as in Muon
O~_t    = O_t - <W_t, O_t>_F / ||W_t||_F^2 * W_t    # the only new line
W_{t+1} = W_t - eta_t O~_t                          # no weight decay
```

`O~_t` is the closest direction to `O_t` that is Frobenius-orthogonal to
`W_t` (they derive it as a bilevel problem). Pythagoras then gives the exact
identity the rest of the paper rests on:

```text
r_{t+1}^2 = r_t^2 + eta_t^2 ||O~_t||_F^2     =>     r_T^2 <= r_0^2 + p sum_t eta_t^2
```

Notes that matter for reimplementation:

- It is one Frobenius projection per matrix, not an iterative QR. "GS" is the
  name of the projection, not a decomposition.
- The projection is applied to the *final* direction, after momentum and the
  approximate orthogonalization. Any later reshaping (row normalization, per-row
  scaling) would reintroduce a radial part, so it must come last.
- No scale-invariance test (AdamP applies the same projection only to weights
  it detects as scale-invariant).
- The paper's MuonGS has **no weight decay on Muon matrices** and no fixed
  radius. Its selling point is that neither `lambda` nor `R` is tuned.

## Experimental Setup (for calibrating our proxy)

- Modded-NanoGPT optimization-benchmark architecture: RMSNorm, RoPE, QK-norm,
  squared-ReLU MLPs, untied embeddings. 160M (12L/768) and 400M (24L/1024).
  QK-norm and RMSNorm make many hidden matrices exactly scale-invariant.
- FineWeb 100B subset, seq 1024, batch 512 sequences, BF16. Budgets of 1-8
  Chinchilla (3-25B tokens at 160M, 8-63B at 400M).
- Muon variants on **hidden matrices only**, Nesterov momentum 0.95. AdamW for
  token embeddings, output head and 1-D parameters, `betas=(0.8, 0.95)`,
  `eps=1e-10`, weight decay `1e-3`.
- Schedule: no warmup, constant for 15% (matrices) / 30% (AdamW group), then
  linear decay to zero.
- LR tuned on a `sqrt(2)` grid per optimizer, model size and duration.
- MoE: 32L/512, 64 experts top-7 + 1 shared, 135M active / 1B total,
  Ultra-FineWeb, 2% warmup then linear decay.

## Reported Results

- MuonGS beats MuonH at the longer horizons; estimated token-equivalent
  speedup over MuonH of about 7-15% for the dense models.
- 20k-step comparison: MuonGS < MuonH < MuonW (best `wd = 0.0125`) < Muon in
  perplexity. MuonW's norm "initially increases and subsequently declines"
  under the linear-to-zero schedule; vanilla Muon's keeps growing.
- 160M model, constant LR 0.005: raising Muon's LR 16x moves `C_20000` only
  from 0.165 to 0.190.

## What The Paper Does Not Test

- **No corrected-decay (MuonC) baseline**, and **no GS + weight-decay hybrid**
  of any kind. The comparison set is Muon, MuonW, MuonH, MuonGS.
- Their conclusion states the open question directly: "Which rate of norm
  growth works best for a given training budget remains an open question."
- Embeddings and the output head are never projected.

## Implications For This Repository

- With GS the norm is no longer steered by the loss at all: it is a
  deterministic function of the schedule. Adding a decay factor `f_t` keeps
  that property, `r_{t+1}^2 = f_t^2 r_t^2 + eta_t^2 ||O~_t||^2`, and turns the
  `sqrt(t)` growth into a saturating one. That is the one-parameter family of
  norm-growth rates the paper leaves open; see
  `workers/claude_muonc_gs/README.md` for the measured answer on our proxy.
- The growth term is second order in `eta_t`. Corrected decay
  (`papers/md/2506.02285v2_corrected_weight_decay.md`) is also second order in
  `eta_t`, standard decay is first order. Only the corrected form gives a
  schedule-independent equilibrium `r* = ||O~|| sqrt(eta_max / (2 lambda))`.
- Our LM proxy differs from theirs in ways that matter for transfer: tied
  embeddings on the Muon path, LayerNorm with gains, no QK-norm, 8K steps.
